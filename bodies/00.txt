Anthropic confidentially filed paperwork with U.S. regulators for a public listing on June 1, 2026, under the proposed ticker symbol ANTH, according to reports. The filing was submitted to the Securities and Exchange Commission under provisions that allow Anthropic to test investor appetite without publicly disclosing detailed financials until closer to the listing date. OpenAI followed with its own confidential filing about a week later, though OpenAI is reportedly leaning toward a 2027 listing rather than racing to market as quickly as Anthropic appears to be, in part because of ongoing legal challenges from co-founder Elon Musk that continue to cast uncertainty over its corporate governance.

By mid-July, bankers were said to be lining up investor meetings for a listing as soon as October, with institutional investor meetings reportedly continuing through the back half of the month. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are said to be leading the underwriting syndicate. The filing comes on the heels of Anthropic's run-rate revenue crossing $47 billion in May, driven heavily by Claude Code, enterprise API usage, and resale arrangements through major cloud hyperscalers. Analysts at Bernstein noted in a July research note that Anthropic's revenue trajectory was "unprecedented for a company of its age," comparing its growth to the fastest-scaling enterprise software companies in history.

The move follows a wave of major AI-adjacent public listings earlier in the year, including SpaceX's IPO in June -- reportedly the largest in history -- which itself reshaped the competitive landscape further when SpaceX used part of its newly raised capital to acquire Cursor for a reported $60 billion shortly after going public. Anthropic's expected valuation at listing has been the subject of considerable debate, with estimates ranging from $900 billion to $1.2 trillion depending on market conditions in October.

Anthropic's IPO roadshow is expected to emphasize the company's distinctive safety-first brand positioning, which has resonated with enterprise customers who view Anthropic as a more reliable long-term partner compared to rivals perceived as more risk-tolerant. Several large financial institutions and healthcare companies have already signaled that they would prefer Anthropic as their primary AI provider specifically because of the company's public commitments to safety research and responsible scaling practices. This brand premium could translate into higher enterprise retention rates and pricing power.

The IPO would represent a watershed moment for the frontier AI industry, which has operated almost entirely in private markets until now. If Anthropic successfully lists, it would be the first pure-play AI model company to go public, setting a benchmark that OpenAI, xAI, and others will be measured against. Market analysts noted that a successful listing could unlock a new wave of public-market investment into AI. However, some observers have cautioned that Anthropic faces risks heading into the public markets, including intensifying competition and reliance on a small number of large enterprise customers.
