TSMC's results also underscored the growing importance of advanced packaging technology -- the process of combining multiple silicon chips into a single, more powerful package -- as a key bottleneck in AI hardware production. The company's CoWoS packaging technology, which is essential for Nvidia's most advanced AI chips, has been operating near full capacity throughout 2026, with wait times for new orders extending to several months. TSMC has announced plans to roughly double its CoWoS capacity by mid-2027, but the gap between demand and supply remains significant.

The geopolitical dimension of TSMC's dominance has not been lost on policymakers. U.S. Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo reiterated in a July speech that the concentration of advanced chip manufacturing in a single geographic location represents a national security vulnerability for every country that depends on semiconductors. TSMC's Arizona facility, which began producing 4-nanometer chips in early 2026, has gradually increased output but remains well below the scale of the company's Taiwanese fabs.
